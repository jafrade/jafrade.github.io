Beyond the arch

Six directions that stay mid-century and Scandinavian without the rainbow-arch, sunset look. Each is shown on two real cards so you can see how the colour changes between projects.

01

Albers squares

Two nested colour blocks rising from the bottom edge, straight and centred, after Josef Albers' Homage to the Square.

Modernist art history. Rigorous, not decorative.

02

Horizon split

The panel splits into two flat tones, and the work sits across the line, like a 1960s record sleeve or a Marimekko print.

The simplest option. Colour does all the work.

03

Off-centre disc

One full circle, top right, with the work overlapping it. A complete circle reads as Japanese and modernist; only the half-circle reads as a sunset.

Most Japandi. Calm, with lots of negative space.

04

Slats

Vertical bands in the project colour, like a teak slat wall or the front of a mid-century credenza.

Furniture rather than graphics. The most textural option.

05

Shoji grid

Hairline rules divide the panel into panes, like a shoji screen, with one pane filled in the project colour.

Architectural. Pairs well with a grid-based layout.

06

Bauhaus stamp

A soft tinted panel, with a tiny composition of circle, square and bar in the corner, like a maker's mark.

The quietest. The shape becomes a signature, not a backdrop.
